# UI Changes Needed — Time Logged
**Source:** 6 internal feedback calls, 2026-07-06 to 2026-07-09 — Aurora Arjonilla, Laura Larrea, Martin Petrov (2026-07-06), Bruno Ribas (2026-07-08), Mercy Mwende, Michael Shipley (2026-07-09) (all Hubstaff CSMs/team leads, acting as test users + customer-feedback proxies)
**Moderators:** Renata Raggio, Kate Kamianets
**Purpose:** Consolidated, actionable UI change list by page/section, for design + dev. Cross-reference: [interview-synthesis.md](interview-synthesis.md) (all 6 sessions, pain-points framing) and each participant's `scoring-*.csv` / `nuggets.csv` for task-level SEQ scores and sourcing.

Legend: 🔴 Must-fix before rollout · 🟡 Should-fix / strong signal · 🟢 Nice-to-have / longer-term

---

## 1. Multi-member page (manager team table)

- 🔴 **Add color coding to the table.** It currently reads as flat/monochrome and is hard to scan. Extend the existing activity-% color convention (e.g. red under 20%) to this table, and also color-code idle time, PTO, and holiday hours. *(Martin)*
- 🔴 **Resolve timezone handling for batch add-time before launch.** Mercy raised an unresolved, concrete question: when a manager adds time in bulk for members across different timezones, does the system apply the org's timezone, the manager's timezone, or each member's own timezone? Her example: an org owner wants everyone's tracked time to land in a specific window (e.g., 3–4pm Manila time); a UK-based manager adds time in bulk for a Kenya-based member — what happens? She called timezone confusion generally "the pain of my existence" in her CSM work, citing a case where she had to convince a customer "the earth is not flat" to explain timezone math. This is a payroll-accuracy and compliance question, not a UX nit — needs an explicit answer (and likely an in-modal timezone banner, similar to the current single-add experience) before batch add-time ships for timezone-diverse orgs. *(Mercy)*
- 🔴 **Confirm multi-member table performance/pagination at high member counts.** Mercy asked directly: with orgs ranging from 500 to 8,000 members, will the table try to load everyone at once (risking slow loads/timeouts), or is there a cutoff/pagination plan? She wasn't sure whether the table would show a stable, complete list or cut off after the first several names. This is an engineering-feasibility question that needs a direct answer from eng, not another usability session. *(Mercy)*
- 🟡 **Add an audit column/filter**: who added/edited time, for whom, and when — mirroring how timesheet approvals show "approved by." Without it, multiple managers editing the same team will get confusing fast. *(Martin, Renata)*
- 🟡 **Add a "me / team" toggle, or default this page to the manager's own data.** Now a repeated ask from 3 of 6 participants, each from a different angle: Bruno is used to a "me / all" toggle in Reports and isn't used to picking himself out of a member list here (he'd also prefer defaulting to everyone rather than defaulting to "me"); Mercy noted the old Time & Activity report let managers filter to just their own data and that ability seems to have disappeared; Michael went furthest — his actual highest-frequency use case on this page is fixing his own missed time entries, not managing his team, and he referenced Hubstaff's old "me and team tab" pattern plus Lattice's "my personal packet / my team's packet" tabs and Deal's default-to-own-data-as-employee model as concrete reference points. Needs a product-scope decision on whether/how to add this — see also Section 8. *(Bruno, Mercy, Michael)*
- 🟢 Selecting a team with no member narrowing applies the action to the *whole* team — this worked and tested well (SEQ 5), but call it out explicitly in the UI/copy so it's not ambiguous. Bruno separately noted that defaulting to "everyone at once," with the option to filter down, is more consistent with the rest of Hubstaff's pages and "way easier." *(Aurora, Bruno)*
- ✅ Team + date-range filtering scored well across the original three sessions (SEQ 5, 5, 5) — no changes needed here.

## 2. Individual member page (single-member detail/timeline view)

- 🟢 **Back-navigation is largely a non-issue — only Martin reported real difficulty (2/5, "I went the wrong way... I took the wrong route").** Laura and Aurora also instinctively tried the member filter/dropdown to get back, out of Reports habit — but unlike Martin, neither reported confusion, and both rated the task a full 5/5. Bruno (5/5), Mercy ("very very easy, same thing that we do"), and Michael (5/5) found the return trivial via the back button itself. This initially looked like a 3-and-3 split, but the ease scores don't support that — 5 of 6 participants found this easy regardless of path taken. Worth a light-touch fix (clearer back-button label, e.g. "Back to multi-member view") rather than a redesign, and worth confirming Martin's experience generalizes before prioritizing further.
- 🔴 **The header-placed Add Time button was missed entirely by Michael on his first attempt.** His eye went to "somewhere middle screen," not the top-right — old muscle memory from where action buttons used to sit ("I'm not looking up here really high top right"). This caused his core "add time for the whole team" task to be scored a Failed outcome (needed multiple prompts to find it); he found the button quickly on a later task once he'd learned where to look. This is a concrete, measured discoverability data point — not just a qualitative worry — and it's the same underlying pattern as the back-navigation issue above: users bring old page-model habits to the new layout. The header pattern needs a stronger visual anchor for users new to it. *(Michael)*
- 🟡 **Give non-tracked days a visual distinction** on the timeline — currently they don't look clickable/interactive. *(Martin)*
- 🟡 **Preserve last-viewed-member/team state** when re-opening timesheets, consistent with today's "shows the last person you checked" behavior. *(Martin, confirmed as intended by Renata)*
- 🟢 **Check permission model for "project viewer" role** — these users can currently see time for anyone on projects they view, not just people they manage. Confirm the new individual-view permission logic (dropdown shown/hidden/disabled) accounts for this edge case. *(Martin)* — flagged as open question, not yet resolved.
- 🟡 **The "keep the timeline as-is" assumption isn't fully settled.** Martin, when asked to choose between timeline vs. multi-date entry, called the timeline intuitive and worth keeping. Bruno independently praised the timeline visuals ("looks good... the bars"). But Michael — the strongest voice on this tradeoff — said he'd trade the timeline away for multi-day entry, and confirmed this preference holds for *both* the single-member and manager/batch context ("the same for both"), not just batch. This directly conflicts with treating "keep the timeline" as settled. *(Martin, Bruno, Michael — see also Section 3's multi-date item)*

## 3. Add Time / Edit Time modal

- 🔴 **Support multiple dates per save — a 3-of-6 direct ask (Aurora, Laura, Michael), with a 4th (Mercy) wanting the same outcome conditioned on undo shipping first.** Aurora and Laura flagged this gap in the original sessions. Michael made the strongest, most detailed case: he wants a true date-range picker (not just repeated single entries) for the common scenario of applying the same consistent hours across many consecutive days — his examples were a team retreat (8 hrs/day × 5 days, one action) and backfilling a full pay period for a member who wasn't onboarded on day one so their first payroll run includes them; he estimated most real bulk cases involve at most 4–5 entries. He also confirmed this preference applies equally to the single-member view, not just batch. Martin, notably, did not raise this — he completed a two-date batch entry without issue. Already flagged internally as "exploring, out of scope for this release" — this is now a stronger signal than when originally scoped. Sequencing note: Mercy is not opposed to date-range entry, but wants it sequenced *after* batch-undo ships (see Section 6) — until then she'd rather keep the safer one-date-at-a-time flow.
- 🟡 **Clarify the "two time entries" / batch-add wording.** Bruno initially misread "two time entries" as meaning one entry each for two members (i.e., two members), not two separate dated entries for one selection. He suggested adding "(s)" near the time-selection label to signal multiplicity is possible — or renaming to something like "Add time in batch," which, once he saw that exact label elsewhere in the flow, read clearly to him immediately ("this is very clear"). *(Bruno)*
- 🟡 **Resolve the single-member vs. batch multi-day inconsistency — this reopens a tradeoff the original sessions thought was settled.** Mercy, coming at it independently as a manager, found it confusing that the single-member Add Time dialog doesn't support multiple days while the batch dialog does — she said she'd expect parity ("I will not understand why this part... is different... I'm still adding for one user"). This complicates the original informal consensus (multi-date for batch, timeline preserved for single-member) — worth revisiting rather than treating as resolved. *(Mercy)*
- 🟡 **Let users switch from single-member to multi-member entry mid-flow, with the original member pre-selected.** Michael's ask: starting from a specific member's Actions > Add Time shouldn't force a separate, disconnected flow if the manager decides they also want to add time for other members — he'd want that member pre-selected in a unified modal rather than backing out and restarting from the multi-member view. *(Michael)*
- 🟡 **Make the time span/date-range field more visually prominent.** Martin: "the time is the most important... for it to be clear where the time has been tracked, where it hasn't." Applies to both the manager and single-member Add Time modal. Renata agreed this is a worthwhile independent improvement.
- 🟡 **Batch add currently only supports one project per action.** Flagged as a real limitation for orgs wanting per-project accuracy across a team, and corroborated by an independent, unprompted behavioral observation from Bruno, who assigned two team members to a single project in the same batch action without any prompt to split by project. Acknowledged as technically harder (overlap checks, overtime-policy jobs) — worth scoping as a fast-follow rather than blocking launch. *(Martin, Bruno)*
- 🟢 Michael also wants a free-text reason field as an option, rather than only a preselected list, when adding time. *(Michael)*
- ✅ Add-time-for-self (regular user) scored 5/5 across all sessions that ran the task, including Bruno and Michael — no changes needed.
- ✅ Modal's member-prefill behavior is consistent between multi-member and single-member contexts (member pre-filled in single-member view) — confirmed as intentional, keep as-is. (Distinct from the multi-day parity gap flagged above, which is about feature availability, not member pre-fill.)

## 4. Navigation / page naming / terminology

- 🔴 **No naming consensus exists across all 6 participants — this needs a dedicated decision process, not more open-ended interviews.** Aurora: parent nav "Time Tracked" next to sub-tab "Time Logged" reads as a confusing duplicate; she'd rename the parent "Tracking." Laura: naming is fine as-is, though customers say "time entries" more casually. Martin: "Time Tracked" is semantically odd since manual time entries aren't technically *tracked*; he'd propose "time breakdown," "overall time analysis," or "time management" — and separately flagged "timesheet" as a fairly US-centric term with less traction internationally. Bruno: "time entries" makes the most sense to him, since "time tracking" and "time log" mean the same thing in his head — he'd group the whole area as "time tracking, time entries, timesheet approvals, time requests." Mercy: "Time Logged" only made sense to her because it was listed first in the nav; she still can't articulate the difference between "time tracked" and "time logged," and preferred the old "View and Edit" because it told her upfront she could edit and split time. Michael gave the most developed argument of anyone tested: make "Time Tracked" the parent header, with "Timesheet" and "Approvals" as sub-labels — he argued at length that customers currently conflate "timesheet" (which they've learned to mean *all* time tracked over a date range) with "timesheet approvals," and that the confusion is about that conflation, not the word itself; he added that Hubstaff may not need the word "timesheet" to appear standalone anywhere at all. Six participants, six different framings, zero convergence — the disagreement itself is the finding. Needs either a forced-choice preference test/card sort, or a product decision grounded in support-ticket terminology data (which Martin is already collecting) — not another round of the same kind of interview.
- 🟡 **Rename the individual/single-member page to disambiguate it from the multi-member view** — e.g. "Individual time logged" or "[Member]'s time logged" — so users know at a glance which view they're in (ties directly into the back-navigation issue in Section 2). *(Martin)*
- 🟡 Users still say "timesheet" out of habit (a manual-time org without approvals enabled will still ask "where's my timesheet?"). Bruno independently confirmed the same underlying confusion from a different angle: he's seen customers go looking for "timesheets" back under Settings because they forgot it moved to the top nav, and separately noted it's not clear to him what other actions (like approvals) are even available from the Timesheets area. Not a blocker, but worth tracking as customer support volume comes in post-launch. *(Martin, Bruno)*
- 🟢 Customers say "time entries" more casually than "time logged" in conversation (Laura), though Laura herself would *not* rename the page — she sees "Time logged" as clearer than the old "View & Edit." Split opinion; low urgency.
- ✅ Renaming away from the old action-based name "View and Edit" is broadly seen as the right call — old name was uniquely bad (only page named after a CRUD action instead of its content). Expect some "where are my View and Edits" support tickets short-term regardless, per Martin's ticket review.

## 5. Work breaks

- 🟡 **Onboarding pop-up should mention work breaks when relevant** — for orgs that have work breaks enabled, the intro modal currently doesn't reference them, which could confuse existing users. Show a break-specific variant of the copy when the org has that setting on. *(Martin)*
- 🟢 General work-break demand is real (Aurora confirmed customers ask for it, though her answer was actually in response to a question about the *batch* gap specifically, and didn't distinguish single vs. batch). Demand for *bulk/batch* work-break entry specifically is weaker but not zero: Laura has never seen a customer request it across multiple members at once, only single backfills; Bruno independently agreed, noting customers today work around the gap using a dummy project as a placeholder; Michael sees "some value" but called it "probably a fringe case," while noting he doesn't know Hubstaff's overall work-break adoption rate. Mercy is the clear exception — she described one specific customer (unnamed) who uses work breaks "seriously" and considers batch entry "very important" to them. Recommend scoping batch work-break entry as a fast-follow rather than a launch requirement, but track Mercy's account rather than treating "no batch demand" as fully closed. *(Aurora, Laura, Bruno, Michael, Mercy)*

## 6. Bulk actions / batch editing safety

- 🔴 **Add undo and an audit log for batch time entries — now a direct ask from 2 of 6 participants, independently, from opposite directions, plus general risk awareness from others.** Martin: if a manager adds time to 20 members and makes a mistake, there's currently no fast way to reverse it — manual one-by-one correction is described as "a nightmare." He cited a live ~3-year-old incident where a manager could delete shifts arbitrarily far back but Hubstaff could only correct within a 30-day window, causing unfixable data loss that had to escalate to engineering. Mercy, independently and without prompting from Martin's session, raised the identical concern from the opposite direction: she said she'd only be comfortable accepting the date-range batch-entry feature (Section 3) *if* a fast, reliable batch-undo/delete existed first — absent that, she'd rather keep the safer one-date-at-a-time flow. This makes undo/audit-log both the single most-repeated systemic ask across all 6 sessions and a hard sequencing dependency for the date-range feature, not just a nice-to-have safety net — treat it as higher-priority and higher-certainty than a typical single-participant ask. Renata compared this to the existing lifecycle-event log on the payments product; acknowledged as unlikely to ship before initial rollout, but should be tracked as a fast-follow given the precedent. *(Martin, Mercy)*
- 🟡 **Clarify overtime/limit warning behavior during batch add.** Warnings currently exist (hover shows which members exceeded limits) but the experience is still rough — flagged by both Martin and Renata as needing refinement, with likely missing edge cases.
- 🟢 Separate, larger issue surfaced but out of scope for this project: time off/holiday hours don't count toward daily/weekly limits, which is a potential inaccuracy vector (a user could log a full day + a holiday and appear to exceed intended hours without a warning). Log as its own follow-up, not part of this rollout.

## 7. Rollout / pre-launch communication

- 🔴 **The in-app "what's new" announcement modal will be skipped — now confirmed in 3 of 6 sessions, with a specific content critique.** Aurora and Laura both said, unprompted, that they'd click through without reading, even though they understood and liked the copy once forced to read it. Mercy's session reinforced this independently and explained *why*: she described the modal text as "a long paragraph... a bit dense," which is why she skipped it immediately — she liked the content once she was made to read it, so the fix is to shorten the copy, not remove the modal. This is the primary current mechanism for warning users — it will not work alone. *(Aurora, Laura, Mercy)*
- 🔴 **Add a pre-launch banner on the old page** ("this page will soon change") — Aurora's suggestion (also relayed from a colleague in Martin's call) as the fix for "customers will feel shocked by the visual change" even though functionality is preserved or improved.
- 🟡 **Produce a short (~1 min) product-recorded rollout video** distributed before launch, rather than relying on in-app messaging — Laura's suggestion, along with sending it via the Success team/inbox.
- 🟡 **Give org owners/managers a self-service opt-in and rollback**, rather than an all-or-nothing forced rollout with support-ticket-only rollback. Martin's core ask: let orgs test the new experience and back out themselves if it doesn't fit their workflow, without contacting support. Ties to the ~10% rollback that already happened post-launch.
- 🟢 Add more in-context education (contextual callouts linking to a support article or short demo) rather than relying on customers self-serving to the knowledge base after confusion. *(Martin)*

## 8. Other requests (exports, filters, scope)

- 🔴 **CSV/download export is missing** from the new page (it existed on the old "View and Edit" page). Currently the plan is to show it disabled with a "coming soon" label. Martin confirmed this is already showing up in live-chat/ticket feedback since the June 24 launch. Laura separately noted usage is likely low-frequency/niche (most exports happen via Reports instead). Mercy strengthens the case that this isn't purely low-value: some organizations export weekly specifically to see start/stop times in the *user's own* timezone (to reconcile against tools like ClickUp or Time Doctor) — a gap the work-break reports don't cover — and she cited a named account that had "a very huge back-and-forth" over exactly this last year. Her read is that "coming soon" is an acceptable near-term stopgap (the Work Sessions report is a viable interim pointer), but the underlying reconciliation need shouldn't stay unshipped indefinitely. *(Martin, Laura, Mercy)*
- 🟡 **Add a "me / team" filter toggle.** Mercy noted the old Time & Activity report used to let a manager filter to just their own data, and that toggle seems to have disappeared; she'd like it applied here too. This is the same repeated ask independently raised by Bruno and Michael from different angles — tracked as a single cross-cutting item in Section 1 rather than being split across pages; flagging here too so it isn't missed in scoping filters/exports work. *(Mercy — see Section 1 for Bruno, Michael)*
- 🟡 **Keep "Calendar" as its own distinct tab rather than making it the weekly view's new default.** Laura, Bruno, and Mercy all independently prefer a dense table over the calendar/grid layout for weekly — customers come here to reconcile numbers, not browse a calendar (Mercy: in her years at Hubstaff, she recalls a customer bringing up the calendar view only about twice, and none have complained about its absence; Bruno: he doesn't like the calendar view "that much" for timesheets). Michael's take adds a specific, actionable structural fix: defaulting the weekly tab to the new grid/calendar view is "a jarring experience" because nothing on screen explains why weekly suddenly looks like a calendar — he'd keep today's three-tab structure (Daily / Weekly / Calendar), with the new grid design living under its own "Calendar" tab instead of replacing weekly's default. Ties directly to the launch-shock/comms risk in Section 7 — if weekly defaults to the calendar view, expect a real wave of "where did the old numbers view go" tickets. *(Laura, Bruno, Mercy, Michael)*
- 🟢 Weekly/monthly aggregate view demand: monthly is real and growing (more orgs moving to monthly payroll cycles), currently scoped as "coming soon," daily + weekly shipping first. Mercy noted monthly would matter more once/if Hubstaff also has monthly limits functionality, which it currently doesn't.
- 🟢 Row-level "View time logged" hover button can get visually crowded by other row icons (PTO/holiday markers) — consider hiding those icons when empty so the hover action stays visually clear. The hover pill itself was independently praised by Bruno and Michael as a clear, discoverable improvement (Bruno contrasted it favorably with Timesheet Approvals, where rows don't feel clickable). *(Laura; praise from Bruno, Michael)*
- 🟢 Confirm batch-delete still works at the individual-user level post-redesign — Mercy asked for explicit confirmation this capability is preserved after the redesign. *(Mercy)*
- 🟢 Bruno flagged a real, recurring customer request for a pay-period filter (not just weekly/monthly) — filtering to the exact timesheet period an org currently has open. Main complication: teams with members on different pay-period schedules. His proposed fix: let managers click into and filter by specific pay periods, greyed out with an explanatory message if the org doesn't have timesheet approvals enabled (a prerequisite for pay periods to exist). Also comes up in Reports, per Bruno. Worth a product-scope decision. *(Bruno)*
- 🟢 Mercy noted customers have separately asked about a way to batch-upload time entries — a related but distinct ask from the export gap above, worth logging alongside it rather than conflating the two. *(Mercy)*

## Cross-cutting notes (not UI, but relevant context)
- Martin's biggest structural concern wasn't the UI itself but *process*: he asked directly whether customers had been consulted before this rollout, and Renata confirmed there hadn't been direct customer discovery or external feedback on this project prior to this round of testing. Martin felt the rollout was rushed with no clear reasoning given for the timeline. He'd like CS/AM involved earlier via structured research recruiting rather than after-the-fact ticket triage.
- **Michael's core "add time for the whole team at once" task was scored a Failed outcome** — he missed the new header-placed Add Time button entirely on first attempt (see Section 2). This is a concrete, measured data point for the launch-shock/discoverability risk flagged in Section 7, not just a qualitative worry: even an experienced Hubstaff CSM with strong platform familiarity missed the new button placement on first exposure.
- Across all 6 sessions, 5 of 6 completed the core "add time to a whole team at once" task successfully or with only minor friction (Aurora, Laura, and Mercy: success; Martin and Bruno: partial, minor friction) — the underlying batch-editing hypothesis remains validated. Michael's Failed first attempt is attributable to button-placement/habit, not comprehension, but is a real signal the header pattern needs a stronger visual anchor for users new to it.
- **Naming has no consensus across all 6 participants — six people, six different framings (see Section 4).** This should be treated as a "needs a dedicated decision" finding, not a signal to run more open-ended interviews — more sessions of the same kind are unlikely to converge on their own.
